03Power & heat

A miner never takes a break

Electricity is the biggest ongoing cost in mining. Here is what your setup uses, what it costs, and how it compares to everyday things.

Compared to an average U.S. home

An average U.S. home uses roughly 860 kWh per month (U.S. EIA ballpark).

It is also a space heater

Nearly all of the electricity becomes heat. Some home miners use that heat to warm their house in winter, and companies sell miners built into space heaters and water heaters.
Efficiency: the number that matters most

Efficiency is measured in joules per terahash (J/TH): the energy it takes to try one trillion guesses. Lower is better. Two machines with the same speed can have very different power bills.

04How mining works

Your iPad runs real SHA-256 below

Miners bundle new transactions into a block, then race to find a number (the nonce) that makes the block's SHA-256 fingerprint start with enough zeros. There is no shortcut: guess, check, and guess again.

Mine a 3-block chainTry it
Try this: Mine all three blocks so they turn green. Then go back to Block 1 and change "Ana pays Ben 5 BTC" to "Ana pays Ben 500 BTC." Watch what happens to every block after it. That broken chain is why miners matter.

Why mining is essential to the network

Six jobs miners do

It decides what is true without a bank

Bitcoin has no central company keeping the official ledger. Miners compete to add the next page of transactions, and everyone accepts the chain with the most work behind it. That replaces the trusted middleman.

It makes cheating very expensive

To rewrite history (for example, to spend the same coin twice) an attacker would have to redo the work for that block and every block after it, faster than everyone else combined.

It confirms and orders transactions

A payment is not final until a miner includes it in a block. Miners also collect the transaction fees users attach, which is why fees rise when the network is busy.

It issues new coins on a fixed schedule

The block reward is the only way new Bitcoin is created. It halves about every four years (50, 25, 12.5, 6.25, now 3.125 BTC) until the total supply reaches 21 million around the year 2140.

It adjusts itself to keep a steady pace

Every 2,016 blocks (about two weeks) the network resets the difficulty so blocks keep arriving about every 10 minutes, no matter how many machines join or leave. More miners means harder puzzles, not faster blocks.

The incentive keeps miners honest

A miner who plays by the rules earns the reward. A miner who tries to cheat wastes huge amounts of electricity on blocks the network rejects. Honesty is simply the most profitable strategy.
